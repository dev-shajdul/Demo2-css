Our Primary Specialism

Ecclesiastical & Heritage Masonry

Most general builders will decline church committee work or listed-building renovations due to conservation officer oversight, lime mortar specifications, and structural risk. Hartley has led specialist church and heritage builds across the Colne Valley since 1998.

PCC & Committee Liaison

We work directly alongside parish church councils, inspecting architects, and diocesan advisory committees. We know the paperwork, the sign-off cadence, and how to work around service schedules.

Lime Mortars & Sourced Stone

No Portland cement shortcuts on historic fabric. We mix hydraulic and non-hydraulic lime mortars on site and source matching local sandstone through established regional quarry contacts.

Structural Timber & Roof Leadwork

Oak collar trusses, rafter repair, lead gutter lining, and belfry louvre rebuilds. Heritage work carried out under single-contract accountability, not handed off to unknown trades.

Ecclesiastical / Heritage

St Jude’s Vestry Extension & Masonry Consolidation

The Problem

Water ingress through 140-year-old crumbling parapet stones had rotted internal wall-plates. A previous contractor's cement mortar patch had accelerated stone spalling.

What Hartley Built

Carefully racked out all cementitious pointing, introduced seasoned Crosby gritstone copings bedded in hydraulic lime, and built an unobtrusive stone-clad choir vestry with hidden rainwater goods.

Outcome: Passed diocesan inspection with zero snags. Completed 4 days ahead of Christmas midnight mass.

Commercial Fit-Out

The Mill House Bistro & Brewery Tap, Colnebridge

The Problem

A derelict waterside woollen store with uneven flagstones, no three-phase supply, and a sagging intermediate timber floor unable to carry commercial brewing vats.

What Hartley Built

Excavated and cast a reinforced concrete raft foundation, introduced structural steel columns hidden within reclaimed timber boxing, and managed the complete fit-out from bar counter to disabled WC access.

Outcome: Delivered on a strict 14-week commercial schedule. Trading commenced on Easter weekend as planned.

Residential Renovation & Extension

Bank End Farmhouse, Marsh End

The Problem

Owners required an open-plan kitchen-living space and master suite without losing the character of a wind-swept moorland stone property.

What Hartley Built

Sourced weathered stone from a nearby demolished barn to mirror the original coursing. Installed triple-glazed Crittall-style doors under a substantial reclaimed timber lintel.

Outcome: Energy efficiency improved from EPC rating E to B. Project completed within £180k fixed sum allowance.
